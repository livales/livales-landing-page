Gift hunting often starts with searching "gift ideas for my partner" and getting lost in the same lists everyone else reads. The result is a safe gift, but rarely one that gets used.

The gifts people love usually aren't the most expensive ones, just the best fit. The steps below help you get there.

## Five steps

1. Listen for small complaints. "My water bottle keeps leaking" or "one side of my headphones is dead" are the most honest gift hints there are. Note them on your phone whenever you hear one.
2. Look at what they use every day. A gift that replaces something old with a better version almost always gets used.
3. Check their wishlist, or ask the people closest to them. Asking a best friend or sibling isn't cheating. It shows you care.
4. Set a budget, then consider chipping in. For a bigger gift, pooling money with friends lets you give what they actually need.
5. Write a specific message. "Happy birthday" is easy to forget. "So you never get soaked waiting for a ride again" sticks.

## Things or experiences?

For someone who has everything, an experience is often more memorable: a cooking class together, concert tickets, or a whole day planned around them. For a practical person, something they use every day feels more thoughtful. There's no answer that's always right. Go back to step one: what have they been telling you?

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a gift you secretly want for yourself.
- Buying at the last minute, when all that's left is the nearest shop.
- Forgetting their size, colour, or phone model. Check quietly first.
- Treating price as a measure of care.

## Start small

Gifts don't have to wait for birthdays. A small thing that answers last week's complaint often feels warmer than one big present a year.
