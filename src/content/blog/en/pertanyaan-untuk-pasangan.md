After a while together, everyday conversation tends to circle the same things: work, what to eat, who's picking whom up. Not because there's nothing left to say, but because there's rarely a reason to start.

The right question can be that reason. The list below has three parts: light ones to warm up, then questions about the two of you, then deeper ones. There's no need to get through them all in one night.

## How to use them

- Pick a relaxed moment, like dinner with phones off the table or a long trip.
- Take turns: one person asks, you both answer.
- Listen all the way through before responding. A follow-up like "and how did that feel?" is often worth more than the next question.
- It's fine to skip a question you're not ready to answer.

## Light, to warm up

1. What food always cheers you up, even on a bad day?
2. If tomorrow were a day off with no plans, what would you do from morning to night?
3. Which song takes you straight back to high school?
4. What small habit of mine do you secretly like?
5. Where would you like us to go together next year?
6. Which film or series would you like to rewatch with me?
7. What's the funniest thing that's happened on one of our outings?
8. If you could pick up one new skill overnight, what would it be?
9. What's the most memorable small gift you've ever received?
10. What does a perfect Sunday look like to you?

## About the two of us

1. What was the first thing you noticed about me?
2. When did you feel closest to me recently?
3. Which of our memories would you like to relive?
4. What small thing do I do that always makes your day better?
5. Where are we most alike, and where are we most different?
6. What habit would you like us to start together?
7. When did you last feel proud of me?
8. What makes you feel cared for, beyond being asked how your day was?
9. If our relationship were a film, what would it be called?
10. What would you like to hear from me more often?

## Going deeper

1. What worries you most about the future, and how can I help?
2. Which value from your family would you like to bring into our relationship?
3. When do you feel least understood, and what do you actually need in that moment?
4. How do you picture our relationship five years from now?
5. Is there something that used to be hard to tell me that you'd like to share now?
6. When we argue, what helps you calm down the most?
7. What does home mean to you?
8. What's a dream you've never said out loud to anyone?
9. What are you grateful for in our relationship this year?
10. Is there a small promise we could make to each other, starting this week?

## After you talk

Write down one or two answers that stayed with you. A few months from now, reading them again can be a sweet surprise. Answers change over time, and that change is part of your story too.
